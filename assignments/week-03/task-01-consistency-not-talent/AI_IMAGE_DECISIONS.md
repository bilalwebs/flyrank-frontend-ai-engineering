# AI Image Decisions

## Image Strategy Overview

The portfolio uses a mixed approach: real screenshots for proof of work, AI-generated visuals only where they support design without distracting from content.

---

## Real Screenshots (Required)

### Project Screenshots

**Why real screenshots are essential:**

1. **Proof of work**: Demonstrates actual implementation
2. **Technical detail**: Shows real UI, not concepts
3. **Credibility**: Recruiters want to see what you built
4. **Specificity**: Each project has unique features to showcase

**Examples:**
- Dashboard interfaces
- Mobile app screens
- Web application features
- Code editor views

### Profile Photo

**Why real photos build trust:**

1. **Personal connection**: People hire people, not resumes
2. **Professionalism**: Shows you take your career seriously
3. **Memorability**: Helps recruiters remember you
4. **Authenticity**: Proves you're a real person

### Certificates & Awards

**Why real certificates matter:**

1. **Verification**: Proves achievements are legitimate
2. **Details**: Shows specific skills and dates
3. **Recognition**: Demonstrates external validation
4. **Trust**: Third-party endorsement of skills

---

## AI-Generated Visuals (Acceptable)

### Hero Background

**Use case**: Abstract geometric patterns or gradients

**Why acceptable:**
- Purely decorative, doesn't represent work
- Adds visual interest without distraction
- Easy to customize and update
- Consistent with brand colors

**Examples:**
- Subtle gradient backgrounds
- Geometric patterns
- Abstract shapes
- Texture overlays

### Section Dividers

**Use case**: Visual separation between content areas

**Why acceptable:**
- Functional design element
- Doesn't convey information
- Consistent across pages
- Easy to maintain

### Placeholder Images

**Use case**: During development or for missing content

**Why acceptable:**
- Temporary solution only
- Should be replaced with real content
- Maintains layout during development
- Clear that it's placeholder

---

## Why Real Work Proof is Better

### Credibility

- **Recruiters want proof**: They've seen enough concepts
- **Implementation details**: Shows you can ship, not just design
- **Problem solving**: Real projects have real constraints
- **Technical depth**: Screenshots reveal architecture decisions

### Specificity

- **Unique features**: Each project has something special
- **Real user interfaces**: Not idealized designs
- **Actual technology**: Shows real frameworks and tools
- **Measurable results**: Real projects have real outcomes

### Trust

- **Authenticity**: No one can claim your real work
- **Detail orientation**: Screenshots show attention to quality
- **Professionalism**: Taking pride in what you've built
- **Transparency**: Nothing to hide behind illustrations

## Image Selection Criteria

### For Project Screenshots

**Must have:**
- Clear, high-resolution capture
- Shows actual interface, not mockup
- Includes relevant UI elements
- Demonstrates key features

**Nice to have:**
- Multiple views (desktop + mobile)
- Before/after comparisons
- User flow demonstrations
- Technical implementation details

### For Profile Photo

**Must have:**
- Professional appearance
- Clear, well-lit photo
- Appropriate for industry
- High resolution

**Nice to have:**
- Friendly expression
- Clean background
- Consistent with brand
- Recent (within 2 years)

### For Certificates

**Must have:**
- Clear, readable text
- Issuing organization visible
- Date of completion
- Your name clearly shown

**Nice to have:**
- High resolution scan
- Relevant to your field
- Recent certifications
- From recognized institutions

---

## Image Quality Standards

### Resolution Requirements

- **Project screenshots**: Minimum 1200px wide
- **Profile photo**: Minimum 400x400px
- **Certificates**: Minimum 800px wide
- **Hero images**: Minimum 1920px wide

### File Formats

- **Screenshots**: PNG for UI, JPEG for photos
- **Profile photo**: JPEG with good compression
- **Certificates**: PNG for text clarity
- **Hero images**: JPEG for photos, SVG for graphics

### Optimization

- **Compression**: Balance quality and file size
- **Lazy loading**: Load images as needed
- **Responsive**: Multiple sizes for different devices
- **Alt text**: Descriptive text for accessibility

## Rejected Image Examples

### Too Decorative

**Example**: Complex AI illustration with excessive detail

**Why rejected:**
- Distracts from content
- Doesn't represent actual work
- Too busy and overwhelming
- Doesn't match clean design philosophy

### Stock Photos

**Example**: Generic developer at computer

**Why rejected:**
- Not authentic
- Doesn't show your actual work
- Recruiters see through stock photos
- Missed opportunity for real proof

### Conceptual Art

**Example**: Abstract representation of "code"

**Why rejected:**
- Doesn't demonstrate skills
- Too vague and generic
- Doesn't help recruiters evaluate
- Missed opportunity for specificity

## Image Placement Strategy

### Home Page

- **Hero**: Professional photo or abstract background
- **Featured projects**: Real project screenshots
- **Skills**: Icons or simple graphics

### Projects Page

- **Project cards**: Real screenshots of each project
- **Detail views**: Multiple screenshots showing features
- **Before/after**: Real comparisons when relevant

### About Page

- **Profile photo**: Real professional photo
- **Timeline**: Icons or simple graphics
- **Skills**: Technology logos (real, not AI-generated)

### Contact Page

- **Background**: Subtle abstract pattern
- **Icons**: Simple, consistent icons
- **Social logos**: Real platform logos

## Maintenance Guidelines

### Updating Screenshots

- **When to update**: After major feature releases
- **How often**: Every 3-6 months
- **Quality check**: Ensure clarity and relevance
- **Consistency**: Maintain similar style and framing

### Replacing AI Images

- **Priority**: Replace with real work when possible
- **Timeline**: As you complete new projects
- **Quality**: Ensure real images meet standards
- **Consistency**: Maintain visual coherence

### Brand Evolution

- **Review annually**: Assess if images still represent you
- **Update as needed**: Keep current with your skills
- **Maintain consistency**: Keep the same approach
- **Document changes**: Track what changed and why
